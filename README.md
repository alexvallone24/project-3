# project-3
 Overview & Purpose:
  In this project, we focused on observing data centered around electric vehicles (EVs) in the United States. The purpose of this topic is to highlight trends when it comes to registered EVs and spark a discussion regarding the future of these cars as they become more popular.

EV Population Data in Washington:
    When looking at Washington state, we created a heatmap to analyze which areas were most dense with EVs. We discovered that the Seattle area appeared to be a hot spot as it had the highest number of recorded EVs in the state. Users can zoom in and out of this map to observe which areas have the most EV activity within the state. Furthermore, we created an interactive webpage that displays a bar graph showing the number of EV registrations per county in Washington. This webpage also contains bar graphs showing EV population by make and model. Dropdown menus were created so users can receive visual data based on which car make they choose.

EV Registration Data Nationwide:
  When analyzing EV registration trends on a national scale, we constructed a pie chart that shows the top 10 states with the most electric vehicles. You can hover over each portion of the chart and see the number of EV registrations as well as the percentage. California holds 49% of the nations EVs with over 1 million registered vehicles. Furthermore, a bubble chart was included to show EV distribution by state. Users can hover over each bubble and see the number of EV registrations recorded in each state.

EV Fuel Stations in the United States:
  Using Javascript, we made a bar graph that shows the top 10 cities in the United States with EV charging stations. According to our findings, Los Angeles has the most fuel stations with over 1,600 scattered around the area. Seattle has the 5th most charging stations in the US with around 700 recorded in the city.
  

Ethical Considerations:
  When looking at EV data in the United States, we made sure to extract datasets from credible sources, such as government catalogs, to ensure we have accurate information. Confidentiality is also important to consider as the datasets include personal information, such as VIN numbers, that could be linked back to specific individuals. Furthermore, representation is crucial in order to prevent biased conclusions from forming. Since we mainly emphasized information centered in Washington, it is important to consider that these trends do not reflect the full picture of EV registration across every other state in the country.
